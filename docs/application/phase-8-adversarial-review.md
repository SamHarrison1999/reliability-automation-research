# Phase 8 adversarial proposal review

## Review purpose

Evaluate the application as a sceptical software-engineering PhD selector
rather than as the author of the project.

## 1. Is the project still too broad?

Assessment:

Substantially improved.

Two RQs now separate technical effects from software-engineering decisions.

Remaining risk:

A later doctorate could still become over-broad if many automation strategies,
fault types or software domains are added.

Control:

The application treats expansion as future versioned work rather than as part
of the first experiment.

## 2. Is the software explicit?

Assessment:

Pass.

OrderFlow is named.

The bounded `order-api`/`inventory` slice, synthetic workload and deployment
context are explicit.

## 3. Is the software origin/selection explained?

Assessment:

Pass.

OrderFlow is purpose-built and selected for:

- deployment independence;
- observable dependency failure;
- reset control;
- deterministic workload;
- experimental manipulability.

The proposal also admits that this reduces external validity.

## 4. Are empirical data sources explicit?

Assessment:

Pass.

Technical events/workload/provenance are distinguished from direct participant
responses.

## 5. Does the proposal confuse manual technical recovery with human behaviour?

Assessment:

Controlled.

The Phase 5 scripted/manual baseline is not presented as a participant
condition.

The future human condition is separately called manual-decision support.

## 6. Does the proposal overclaim the pilot?

Assessment:

Pass.

It reports execution counts as feasibility evidence and explicitly rejects
statistical-significance, production-readiness and universal-effect claims.

## 7. Is the literature gap overstated?

Assessment:

Controlled but remains a weakness.

The full Phase 1 review is incomplete.

The proposal describes the gap as preliminary and does not claim systematic
coverage.

This should be strengthened further before or during doctoral registration.

## 8. Can RQ2 be answered by the proposed 8-12 participants?

Assessment:

Not by the feasibility sample alone.

The proposal correctly treats 8-12 participants as protocol feasibility.

A later main study requires separate sample-size/analysis justification.

## 9. Is integration between RQ1 and RQ2 credible?

Assessment:

Yes if interpreted as a mixed/socio-technical synthesis rather than pooled
metrics.

Technical recovery times and participant decision measures must remain
separate.

## 10. Is supervisor fit established?

Assessment:

School-level fit is credible.

Individual supervisory fit is not yet confirmed.

Dr Douce should not be presented as an agreed supervisor.

Potential complementary expertise exists in adaptive/resilient and empirical
software engineering within the School, but current willingness/availability
must be established directly.

## 11. Is ethics status clear?

Assessment:

Pass.

Phase 6 is protocol-ready, not ethics-approved.

No participant evidence is claimed.

## 12. Does the application explain candidate suitability?

Assessment:

Pass.

Qualifications, professional technical experience and completed preparation
are directly connected to feasibility of the proposed work.

## Residual risks to discuss with prospective supervisors

- full scoping review remains incomplete;
- OrderFlow external validity;
- main participant sample/design remains to be justified after feasibility;
- integration strategy needs refinement during the doctorate;
- supervisory team is not confirmed;
- application route must be re-verified before submission.

## Review outcome

No mandatory methodological rewrite is required before supervisor circulation.

The draft proposal can be frozen as the Phase 8 application version while
retaining the residual limitations above.

Future supervisor feedback should be captured as a new revision rather than
silently replacing this version.
