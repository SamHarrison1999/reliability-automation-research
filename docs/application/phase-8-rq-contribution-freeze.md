# Phase 8 research-question and contribution freeze

## Final application RQs

### RQ1

Under controlled fault scenarios in a reproducible transactional service
system, how do different recovery-automation strategies affect recovery time,
failure impact and required intervention?

### RQ2

How does automation-assisted incident response influence software engineers'
diagnosis, recovery and escalation decisions during structured
continuous-delivery tasks?

## Why two questions

The two-question structure separates:

- system behaviour; from
- engineer behaviour.

It removes reproducibility as a broad standalone RQ.

Reproducibility instead becomes a methodological contribution supporting both
empirical questions.

## Intended doctoral contributions

### C1 ? reproducible socio-technical research method

A versioned experimental method linking controlled cloud-native faults,
recovery automation and direct human decision evidence without conflating the
measurement streams.

### C2 ? bounded technical evidence

Evidence describing how recovery-strategy effects vary across controlled fault
conditions and outcome definitions.

This is not framed as a search for universal automation superiority.

### C3 ? engineering-practice evidence

Direct evidence of how automation assistance changes diagnosis, recovery,
escalation and reliance decisions in structured incident tasks.

### C4 ? integration guidance

An empirically grounded account of when technical automation outcomes and human
decision evidence agree, diverge or reveal different aspects of reliability.

## Excluded contribution claims

The application does not promise:

- production-system generalisation from OrderFlow alone;
- a universal optimal automation strategy;
- human cognitive effects inferred from telemetry;
- participant findings before ethics-approved data collection; or
- completion of the full systematic/scoping review before it occurs.
