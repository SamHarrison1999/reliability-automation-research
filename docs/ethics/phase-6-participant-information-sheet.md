# Participant Information Sheet - protocol draft

**Study:** Reliability Automation and Software Engineering Practice

**Status:** Draft for future ethics submission

**Do not distribute for recruitment.**

The institutional ethics reference and approved researcher/supervisor contact
details must be inserted using the then-current institutional template before
this document is used.

## Why is the study being planned?

The research examines how software practitioners diagnose software-service
incidents and choose recovery or escalation actions when different forms of
automation support are available.

## Why might I be invited?

The planned study recruits adults with relevant professional software,
DevOps/SRE, platform, cloud or production-support experience.

## What would taking part involve?

The planned remote session lasts approximately 60-75 minutes.

You would:

1. read the study information and confirm consent;
2. answer a short professional-experience questionnaire;
3. complete four simulated OrderFlow incident tasks;
4. make a diagnosis and recovery/escalation decision for each task;
5. provide a short typed explanation;
6. rate workload after each task; and
7. answer short questions about the automation and the study experience.

Some tasks include an automation recommendation.

You will be told in advance that recommendations may be incorrect.

## Is this a test of me?

No.

The study is research into the task and automation design.

It is not a job interview, certification test or assessment of your
professional competence.

## What systems would I access?

Only a controlled research interface using synthetic OrderFlow incidents.

You would not access:

- production systems;
- real customer data;
- real payments;
- unrestricted Kubernetes infrastructure; or
- employer systems.

## Recording

The initial feasibility protocol does not collect webcam, audio, video or
screen recordings.

## Risks or inconvenience

The anticipated burden is similar to completing structured technical reasoning
tasks.

You may find some tasks uncertain or mildly frustrating.

You can stop at any point without giving a reason.

## Benefits

There is no guaranteed personal benefit.

The research may improve understanding of how reliability automation should be
evaluated alongside software-engineering decision-making.

## Compensation

The application-stage plan is a GBP 20 digital voucher for the approximately
60-75 minute session.

Payment is for time, not performance.

The final amount, funding source and payment procedure must be approved and
stated in the ethics-approved version before recruitment.

## What information would be collected?

The research record may include:

- a pseudonymous participant identifier;
- broad professional-experience categories;
- task decisions and timings;
- workload ratings;
- automation-related responses;
- typed task explanations; and
- study feedback.

Recruitment/contact information and consent documentation are stored separately
from task responses.

## What should I avoid writing?

Do not enter:

- employer or customer names;
- confidential system details;
- personal names;
- passwords;
- API keys; or
- other secrets.

## Confidentiality

Research responses will use a study pseudonym.

Identifiable and pseudonymised participant data will not be committed to the
public GitHub repository.

Only appropriately anonymised/aggregate outputs may be publicly disseminated
after the approved disclosure process.

## Withdrawal

The planned withdrawal window is 14 calendar days after participation.

A withdrawal request made during that period will be actioned using the
separately held participant-to-study-ID link.

After the declared anonymisation boundary, it may no longer be possible to
identify and remove an individual's data.

The ethics-approved version will state the final withdrawal procedure.

## Voluntary participation

Participation is voluntary.

You may decline or stop without giving a reason and without effect on your
employment, education or professional standing.

## Ethics status

This repository copy is a protocol draft.

The study must not recruit participants until the required favourable ethics
opinion and other relevant approvals have actually been obtained.
